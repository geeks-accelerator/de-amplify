                                   3           The parties do not dispute the legal standard for attorney-client privilege. “The attorney-

                                   4   client privilege protects confidential communications between attorneys and clients, which are

                                   5   made for the purpose of giving legal advice.” In re Grand Jury, 23 F.4th 1088, 1091 (9th Cir.

                                   6   2021) (quoting United States v. Sanmina Corp., 968 F.3d 1107, 1116 (9th Cir. 2020)). The Ninth

                                   7   Circuit has directed courts to apply the “primary purpose test” which requires a court to consider

                                   8   “whether the primary purpose of the communication is to give or receive legal advice, as opposed

                                   9   to business” advice. Id.1

                                  21             The standard for attorney-client privilege in the Ninth Circuit is different than in other
                                       jurisdictions, including California and other federal circuits. Here, the privilege is “narrowly and
                                  22   strictly construed.” United States v. Gray, 876 F.2d 1411, 1415 (9th Cir. 1989) (citing Weil v.
                                       Inv./Indicators, Research & Mgmt., Inc., 647 F.2d 18, 24 (9th Cir. 1981)). Importantly, the Ninth
                                  23   Circuit has explicitly rejected the “because of” test, which looks at whether a dual-purpose
                                       communication was made in anticipation of litigation, finding it too broad for the principles
                                  24   behind the attorney-client privilege and therefore more suited for the work-product doctrine. In re
                                       Grand Jury, 23 F.4th at 1093-94. Although Grand Jury left open the question of whether legal
                                  25   advice must be “the primary purpose” or just “a primary purpose,” the Ninth Circuit has since
                                       declined to apply the test in the latter manner and instead endorsed the former test such that legal
                                  26   advice must be more than merely “a primary purpose” for the privilege to attach. Epic Games,
                                       Inc. v. Apple Inc., 161 F.4th 1162, 1179 (9th Cir. 2025).
