                                  1    II.    LEGAL STANDARD AND ANALYSIS
                                  2           Approval of a proposed consent decree is within the sound discretion of the Court. United
                                  3    States v. Oregon, 913 F.2d 576, 580 (9th Cir. 1990); SEC v. Randolph, 736 F.2d 525, 529 (9th Cir.
                                  4    1984). It is well-settled that the Court may approve a consent decree if it determines that “it is fair,
                                  5
                                       reasonable and equitable and does not violate the law or public policy.” Turtle Island Restoration
                                  6
                                       Network v. U.S. Dep't of Com., 672 F.3d 1160, 1165 (9th Cir. 2012). The Court must “be satisfied
                                  7
                                       that the decree represents a ‘reasonable factual and legal determination’” Oregon, 913 F.2d at 581,
                                  8
                                       independently scrutinize a proposed consent judgment's terms, and “eschew any rubber stamp
                                  9
                                       approval.” United States v. Montrose Chem. Corp. of Cal., 50 F.3d 741, 747 (9th Cir. 1995); see
                                  10
                                       also Local No. 93, Int'l Ass'n of Firefighters v. City of Cleveland, 478 U.S. 501, 525 (1986) (“[A]
                                  11
                                       federal court is more than a recorder of contracts from whom parties can purchase injunctions; it is
                                  12
Northern District of California




                                       an organ of government constituted to make judicial decisions.”).
 United States District Court




                                  13
                                              The Court has considerable experience with the legal and factual underpinnings of this
                                  14

                                  15
                                       action, including the strengths and weaknesses of each party’s arguments and factual proffers.

                                  16   Given the disputes regarding the precise statutory meaning of COPPA, the Court does not find on

                                  17   this record that the Settlement Agreement violates its terms, nor does it violate any other law or

                                  18   public policy raised before the Court. Rather, the fundamental concerns driving this litigation are

                                  19   technologically complex, as are the public policies attempting to regulate corporate conduct,
                                  20   especially conduct which impacts adolescents. The Court finds that the Settlement Agreement
                                  21   between the parties reflects a fair, reasonable, comprehensive, and good faith approach not only to
                                  22   provide monetary relief, but importantly, to change conduct in a way that attempts to meaningfully
                                  23   address the negative impacts of the social media platforms at issue. Further, given the rapidly
                                  24
                                       changing technology, the ability for parties to work collectively, with the assistance of independent
                                  25
                                       experts, is a much more efficient use of resources. Accordingly, the Court approves the Settlement
                                  26
                                       Agreement as set forth herein.
