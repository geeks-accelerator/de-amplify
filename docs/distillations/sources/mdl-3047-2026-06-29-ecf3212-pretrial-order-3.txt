                                  23             2.    Motion for Relief from Discovery Order (Dkt. No. 3091): Plaintiffs challenge
                                  24   Magistrate Judge Kang’s discovery orders issued on January 12, 2026 and May 18, 2026 regarding
                                  25   redactions set in four documents.
                                  26             A motion for relief from a non-dispositive order should only be granted when the moving
                                  27   party establishes that the non-dispositive order by the magistrate judge is clearly erroneous or
                                  28   contrary to law. Fed. R. Civ. P. 72; see Bhan v. NME Hospitals, Inc., 929 F.2d 1404, 1414 (9th
                                       Case 4:22-md-03047-YGR            Document 3212         Filed 06/29/26       Page 2 of 6




                                   1   Cir.1991) (“the magistrate's decision on a nondispositive issue will be reviewed by the district

                                   2   judge under the clearly erroneous standard”); Barnes & Noble, Inc. v. LSI Corp., No. C–11–2709

                                   3   EMC, 2013 WL 841334, at *1 (N.D.Cal. Mar. 6, 2013) (same). “In finding that the magistrate

                                   4   judge’s decision is ‘clearly erroneous,’ the Court must arrive at a definite and firm conviction that a

                                   5   mistake has been committed.” Barnes & Noble , 2013 WL 841334 at *1 (internal citations and

                                   6   quotations omitted). This standard of review is extremely deferential. Id.

                                   7           The Court finds that the State AGs have not satisfied their burden under Rule 72(a) of

                                   8   Federal Rules of Civil Procedure to show that Magistrate Judge Kang’s order was “clearly

                                   9   erroneous or [] contrary to law.” In fact, their arguments misconstrue the record. Magistrate Judge

                                  10   Kang applied the correct law. Whether this Court would have found differently is not the issue.

                                  11   Given his lengthy decisions and considered view of the record over months of briefing, the Court

                                  12   declines to overturn the decision. The motion is denied.

                                   4   way of background, the Court authorized the videotaping of the trial testimony of Mark Zuckerberg

                                   5   to preserve the testimony specifically for use in other AG trials in this MDL. Further, the AGs

                                   7   evidence was the same. The Court declined that invitation given the failure of the AGs to settle on

                                   8   jury instructions with core issues identified across all claims.

                                   9            Now, having seen the AGs’ actual trial plan and witness designations, and given the lack

                                  10   of a binding jury trial, there is no need for successive trials repeating the same evidence. Thus,

                                  11   there is no need to preserve on video Mr. Zuckerberg’s testimony for future AG proceedings and

                                  12   the Court will not so order. This order does not apply to Mr. Zuckerberg’s testimony in non-AG
