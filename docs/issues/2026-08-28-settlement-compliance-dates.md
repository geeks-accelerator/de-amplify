# The consent judgment has a compliance calendar, and nothing on the site will notice when its dates arrive

The settlement's obligations run from defined dates, all derivable from the entered judgment
(Effective Date 2026-08-27, the first business day after entry):

| Date | What is due |
|---|---|
| 2026-09-26 | Initial Guaranteed Installment Payment (30 days from Effective Date), per state whose consent judgment is entered |
| ~2026-09-10 | Joint motions due in the fifteen Satellite AG Actions (ten business days from the agreement's notice) |
| 2026-10-27 | Independent Auditor's term begins (two months from Effective Date) |
| 2026-12-27 | Non-Personalized Feed option must be offered (four months from Effective Date) |
| 2027-02-27 | Compliance Date: the Phase I time-management defaults and the rest of the injunctive terms |
| ~2028 | First Auditor executive summary becomes public (first report covers the first ~four quarters, plus drafting rounds) |

**Why this is an issue and not a ledger Tension:** each individual fact belongs to the mdl-3047
ledger and section (k) carries the defined dates. What no ledger owns is the standing obligation to
*look* when the dates arrive, which is exactly the class of thing this directory exists for: the
site's most likely failure a year from now is describing the ordered brake while the shipped brake
quietly diverges from it, and the first executive summary is the one public document that will say
so. The scorecard's precedent note (added 2026-08-28) is the surface that would need the honest
downgrade if Meta ships something weaker than the order.

**What "looking" means, concretely:** on the Compliance Date, run the scorecard's seven questions
against the actual shipped teen experience on Instagram and Facebook and record the result in the
ledger; when the executive summary publishes, cache it and tier its findings. Both are one-session
tasks with a primary source at the end of them.
