# The Doe 1 en banc petition is undecided, and section 4 of the paper depends on which way it goes

The paper's section 4 rests on *Doe 1 v. Meta Platforms* (9th Cir., No. 24-1672, April 28, 2026),
which held that "matching users with content is publishing conduct" and so Section 230 bars
engagement-driven recommendation claims. **That is the current law of the circuit and the circuit is
visibly trying to undo it**: Judge Berzon, joined by Judge W. Fletcher, urged reconsideration en
banc, and the author of the opinion wrote that "it will fall to the en banc court" to settle it.

The plaintiffs petitioned on 2026-05-12, the panel ordered Meta to respond the same day, and the
petition was **fully briefed and undecided as of the docket on 2026-08-10**.

**This is the single legal fact most likely to change what section 4 says.** If rehearing is granted,
the paper's characterisation of recommendation-as-publishing becomes provisional in a way the
current text does not convey. The text already hedges correctly ("build on it as current law, not as
settled law"), which is why this is blocked rather than open: nothing needs writing until the court
acts.

Note the neighbouring holding, which is not the same thing and should not be conflated with it. The
Ninth Circuit's published opinion of 2026-08-10 in No. 24-7032 holds Section 230 is a defense to
liability rather than immunity from suit. That is about **appealability**, is already in the paper,
and does not turn on this petition.
