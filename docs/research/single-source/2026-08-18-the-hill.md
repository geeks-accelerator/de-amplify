## Step 1: Structure

The structural point matters more than usual here. A live blog is not one document; it is a stack
of timestamped micro-documents by different authors, sharing a page. The quasi-independent-source
insight applies almost literally rather than by analogy, and the correct extraction unit is the
individual post.

1. Page summary paragraph (staff)
2. Post, 4 hours ago: states deliver opening argument (Shapero)

## Step 5: Tensions (internal)

1. **H5 and H6 against H7 and H14.** Meta's counsel concedes age verification is genuinely hard and industry-wide, then asserts a policy premised on knowing who is under 13, then denies knowingly allowing under-13s. Held together this is coherent as advocacy (hard, we try, we do not knowingly fail), but the concession and the denial are doing opposite work and the blog does not remark on it. **This is the most useful internal tension in the batch**, because it is Meta's own brake, conceded to be unreliable by its own counsel, in open court.
2. **The summary paragraph says "several states"; post 4 says four; H10 says 29 in coalition.** Three different counts on one page, all technically defensible, none reconciled.

## Step 6: Synthesis

**S1. The question presented, stated plainly by the states' own advocate.**
Did Meta mislead the public about risks to kids, did it act unfairly, and did it violate the
children's privacy statute. *Basis: COURTROOM.* Three questions, not one, and a partial answer is
therefore possible.

**S2. Meta's counsel conceded in open court that age verification is a real and industry-wide problem.**
*Basis: COURTROOM.* On this site's terms that is a concession that a specific brake does not
reliably work, made by the party that built it. It sits directly against the same counsel's
assertion that the platform has always required users to be over 13.

**S3. A live blog is a source class that needs per-post extraction.**
Three posts by two authors at three times, inside a page about primary elections. Treating the
page as one document would have blurred authorship and timestamps and lost H5 entirely.

Orphans: the entire non-Meta content of the page, which is large and correctly out of scope. Within

## Step 8: Quality

Moderate as reporting, high in value for one reason: it is the only source in the batch that
captured Meta's age-verification concession. That is a function of the format. A live blog records
what was said as it is said, without the compression that a finished article imposes, and the
compression is what dropped this elsewhere.
