## Step 5: Tensions (internal)

1. **P2 against the rest of the batch.** NPR states as fact that the four states are seeking up to $1.4 trillion. Reuters, CNBC and this project's own ledger all trace that figure to Meta's filing. Within the article there is no contradiction, which is exactly why it is dangerous: it reads as settled.
2. **P12 versus P26.** The court has already cited Meta's own documents as supporting the PR-stunt theory, while Meta calls the claims unsubstantiated. Both are reported without noting that the first is a judicial finding on a summary-judgment record and the second is advocacy.
3. **P10 sits alone.** The only doctrinal scepticism in a piece otherwise structured around the plaintiffs' theory.
4. **The NPR disclosure (P22)** is properly placed but never revisited.

## Step 6: Synthesis

**S1. This is the clearest published statement of the design theory and its mechanism.**
Features built to hold attention, monetised through advertising, with the harm running through
time-on-platform. *Basis: RECORD.* Better articulated here than in the courtroom coverage.

**S2. The June ruling already put brake efficacy inside the case.**
A judge cited Meta's documents as supporting the theory that time-restriction tools were a public
relations stunt. *Basis: RECORD.* This project already carries this claim from the primary order;
NPR corroborates it independently.

**S3. The doctrinal risk is that design and content cannot be separated.**
If the design claims cannot be disentangled from content claims, the Section 230 avoidance fails.
*Basis: EXPERT.* The strongest stated argument against the plaintiffs' theory in the batch.

**S4. A widely trusted outlet misattributed the trillion-dollar figure to the states.**
*Basis: REPORTER, and it is wrong.* Recorded as a finding about the coverage rather than about the
case, because it is direct evidence that this project's existing framing is the more accurate one.
