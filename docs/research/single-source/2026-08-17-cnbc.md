## Step 5: Tensions (internal)

1. **B17 versus B18.** The states in this case ask for removal of infinite scroll and engagement-optimized algorithms. A judge in the analogous New Mexico case declined to order exactly those. The article places both without connecting them. This is the most useful unremarked tension in the entire batch.
2. **B23 ("stock is down 11% this year") versus the CNBC television segment from the same day**, which says down about 28% in the past year. Different windows, easily conflated.
3. **The framing "if New Mexico created the blueprint" versus B18**, which establishes that the blueprint has holes precisely where this site's thesis lives.
4. **Torrez is quoted on the stakes of a case he is not party to** (B28), with no counterweight beyond the Meta statement.

## Step 6: Synthesis

**S1. The brake remedy has a judicially established ceiling.**
A New Mexico court declined to order elimination of infinite scroll or changes to recommendation
algorithms, on separation-of-powers grounds, on Section 230 and First Amendment grounds, and on the
ground that rivals not before the court would keep the features. *Basis: RECORD via reporter.*

**CORRECTED 2026-08-18. This output originally carried the tag NEEDS-PRIMARY and called itself
"the highest-value claim in the batch for this project". It was neither open nor new.** The
holding was already established, already verified against the primary, and already published, at
`content/lawsuits/new-mexico-v-meta.md`, in `content/lawsuits.md`, and as claim 36 of
`docs/distillations/new-mexico-v-meta.md`. The primary was already cached in this repository, and
reading it directly showed the court's reasoning at paragraphs 160 to 166 is richer than the
reporting distilled here.

The entry is kept rather than deleted, because the error is the useful part. A single-source
distillation is built from one document and is **structurally blind to the project's existing
record**. That this file independently surfaced a fact the project already held is a mild positive
signal about the method. That it labelled it an open research question is a strong negative signal
about reading a `NEEDS-PRIMARY` tag as a work queue. See the dedupe rule in the README.

**S2. The states ask for algorithmic deletion, not only data deletion.**
Under-13 data plus the algorithms and models trained on it. *Basis: RECORD via reporter.* A
materially different and larger remedy than a data purge.

**S3. The $200 billion was said to the judge, last week, by the states' own lawyers.**
*Basis: COURTROOM.* This is the most specific sourcing of the figure in the batch, naming the
audience and the timeframe, and it corroborates Reuters independently.

**CONTESTED 2026-08-18 by the primary, and this output should not be relied on.** The states' own
filing of 2026-07-13, docket entry 473, uses "$200 billion" for **Meta's 2025 revenue**, cited to
Meta's Form 10-K, and states that the AGs will present their final requests for monetary relief at
trial. No filing reached in that pass states $200 billion as a demand. Either the figure was
stated separately at an August hearing, whose transcript is not mirrored in the free archive, or
the coverage read a revenue figure as a demand. Unresolved.

**S4. New Mexico's actual order is a concrete brake specification.**
Age assurance using AI, an under-13 prediction model inside two years, a cross-platform reporting
portal with schools. *Basis: RECORD via reporter.* This is what a court-ordered brake looks like
when it is granted rather than refused, and it pairs with S1 as the two halves of the ceiling.
